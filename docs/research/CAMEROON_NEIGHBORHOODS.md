# Cameroon Neighborhoods: World Map Reference

Research dossier for NDAMBA's world map. Compiled 2026-10-31 from French and English Wikipedia, osidimbea.cm (local-government data), Cameroon Tribune, camer.be, 237online, le360 Afrique, VOA, Africanews, expat and travel guides. "Verified" means the name appears as a quartier, village or locality in an official or encyclopedic source; "unverified" means common usage only.

## Yaoundé (Centre Region, about 700 m, "ville aux sept collines")

Hills, red earth, two rainy peaks (May and October) inside a March to November wet season, drier December to February. Stade Omnisports Ahmadou Ahidjo (opened 1972, about 40,000 seats, natural grass) is in Mfandena, Yaoundé V; home of Canon Yaoundé and the national team. Arrondissement numbers below for Yaoundé I, IV and V entries come from Wikipedia pages; those given for Yaoundé II and VI follow common usage because the Yaoundé VI page could not be fetched.

- Mokolo (Yaoundé II). Verified. Central-west commercial quarter with the city's largest market, roads shared by vendors and vehicles, very mixed population. Flagship market level.
- Mvog-Ada (Yaoundé V). Verified. Densely populated, lively, bars and nightclubs, two markets, Samuel Eto'o listed among people associated with it; the "Montée Caveau" nightlife hill.
- Essos (Yaoundé V). Verified. Large, lively popular quarter in the center-east, snack bars, taxi culture, the 1972 Camp Sonel estate at Essos-Mfandena.
- Nlongkak (Yaoundé I, seat of the commune). Verified. Central, administrative, Centre culturel camerounais; "Bata Nlongkak" named after a Bata shoe shop at the crossroads.
- Biyem-Assi (Yaoundé VI). Verified. Dense residential area grown from a late-1970s MAETUR housing scheme ("Maisons Blanches"); named after the Biyeme stream.
- Etoudi (Yaoundé I). Verified as a locality: Chefferie Emana-Etoudi, presidential palace, SODEPA abattoir and cattle market at the northern entrance, bus station for the West, busy nightlife. Not a standalone quartier on the Yaoundé I list.
- Emana (Yaoundé I). Verified. Northern quarter, "Borne Fontaine" public fountains, Santa Barbara residential zone on its edge.
- Mendong (Yaoundé VI). Verified on the Yaoundé page: southwestern, grew around an early-1980s camp, gendarmerie and police posts, relatively calm. Not on the Yaoundé VII list.
- Obili (Yaoundé VI). Verified on the Yaoundé page: western, IRIC institute, students, many bars and shops, English speakers.
- Nkolndongo, also spelled Nkoldongo (Yaoundé IV). Verified under the Nkolndongo spelling (camer.be: "hill of Ndongo"; district hospital; Mbolé birthplace per Cameroon Tribune, Africanews and VOA). Use Nkolndongo as canonical.
- Melen (Yaoundé VI). Verified. Ewondo word for oil palm; 1960s market gardening; university and polytechnic area.
- Ekounou (Yaoundé IV). Verified. Eastern, about 54,000 people in 2005, big market demolished in April 2022 with a new site planned, bilingual lycée.

## Douala (Littoral Region, sea level, Wouri estuary)

About 3,600 mm of rain a year, wettest July to September, recurrent flooding May to October. Two bridges to Bonabéri. Stade de la Réunification (Bépanda, 1972, about 30,000 seats, grass) hosts Union Douala, Les Astres and others; AFCON 2021 training venue only.

- Bépanda (Douala V, a prefix for several quartiers: Bépanda Omnisports, Bépanda Voirie, Bépanda Yonyong). Verified. Very dense, University of Douala, the Réunification stadium. Natural "stadium district" level.
- New Bell (Douala II). Verified. Historic, multiethnic, the central market, the central prison, 1913 to 1914 resistance history, Bamiléké, Bassa, northern and Anglophone communities.
- Deïdo, also Deido (Douala I). Verified. Riverside, the Rond-Point Deïdo traffic hub, "La Nouvelle Liberté" statue, Deïdo chiefdom, youthful nightlife, club Léopard Sportif de Douala.
- Akwa (Douala I). Verified. Business and nightlife heart: shops by day, bars and street food by night, German-era buildings, expensive leases, potholed streets in the rains.
- Bonabéri, also Bonaberi (Douala IV). Verified. Right bank of the Wouri, ZIBO industrial zone (cement, steel, brewing), mixed informal housing, gateway to the West and the Anglophone regions.
- Bonamoussadi (Douala V, "Bonamoussadi Cité"). Verified. Planned mid-range residential subdivisions, daily market, new malls and supermarkets; the Douala V town hall.
- Makepe, also Makèpè (Douala V: Makèpè Maturité, Petit Pays, Recasement, Terminus, Cité Makepé). Verified. Northern residential area with a chicken market.
- Logpom (Douala V). Verified as both quartier and village; residential, near Bépanda Yong-Yong.
- Bonapriso (Douala I). Verified. The wealthiest, expat-favored district: perpendicular streets, flower market, consulates, highest rents.
- Nylon (Douala III). Verified. Peripheral popular zone built on waterlogged land since the 1960s, about 200,000 people on 700 ha, spontaneous housing, flooding, strong neighborhood committees.

## Secondary cities

Bafoussam (West, about 1,420 m, Bamiléké plateau): Banengo (hill the city spreads over, technical lycée), Djeleng (health district centre), Tamdja (parish, name only), Bamendzi (2,000-seat municipal stadium). All verified. Stade Omnisports de Kouekong (20,000, 2016) is the big ground.

Bamenda (North-West, about 1,500 to 1,600 m, Anglophone, Pidgin on the street, cool, about 2,260 mm): Mankon (Bamenda II, Fon's palace and museum), Nkwen (Bamenda III), Up Station (administrative plateau above the escarpment), Ntarikon (SDF launch site, 1990). All verified. PWD Bamenda won Elite One in 2019-20.

Buea (South-West, about 870 m on Mount Cameroon, fog and drizzle, former colonial capital, University of Buea): Molyko (university quarter), Great Soppo, Small Soppo, Bonduma, Mile 17, Muea. Verified as names; Molyko stadium not confirmed.

Limbe (South-West coast, black volcanic sand, SONARA refinery, botanic garden): Down Beach (beach fish market, catch grilled on the spot; verified by travel sources, not Wikipedia), Bota (CDC head office), Mile 4 (sunset viewpoint).

Kribi (South coast, white sand, Lobé falls, deep-sea port, September wettest): Kribi I (Massaka) and Kribi II (Dombé) are the administrative halves; Grand Batanga, Mboa-Manga and Afan-Mabé are commonly cited but unverified here.

Garoua (North, Bénoué river, cotton, hot, no rain December to February, Harmattan haze):
- Roumdé Adjia: quarter and 25,000-seat stadium, Coton Sport (Elite One since 1993), AFCON 2021 venue. Verified.
- Poumpoumré, Lopéré: verified as names.
- Yelwa: northwestern suburb near the airport. Verified.

Maroua (Far North, hot semi-arid, about 794 mm, Fulfulde, moto-taxis, tree-lined avenues, tanners and potters):
- Domayo, Djarengol, Kakataré, Doualaré, Palar (one of the largest), Hardé, Dougoï. Verified as localities.

Ngaoundéré (Adamawa plateau, rail terminus from Douala, Lamidat, cattle): Bamyanga (several sub-quarters) and Bali verified; Dang (university) and Joli Soir are widely used but unverified here; Mardock is a wooded site.

Bertoua (East, forest region capital, 1,500 to 2,000 mm): Madagascar verified as a village of Bertoua I; Nkolbikon, Tigaza and Enia unverified.

Kumba (South-West, Lake Barombi Mbo crater lake, market hub): Fiango (hilly, Fiango Market), Kosala, Mbonge Road (hilly, market), Buea Road (flat), Three Corners (market), Mambanda, Kake. All verified.

Ebolowa (South, equatorial forest, Bulu heartland, cocoa): New-Bell verified (flood-prone, marshy); Angalé and Nko'ovos unverified here; Mekalat verified as village names.

Dschang (West, about 1,400 m, cool resort climate, University of Dschang, Musée des civilisations): Tsinkop, Ngui, Mingmeto, Keuleng, Fiala (Foto) II verified; Paid Ground unverified.

Foumban (West, about 1,180 m, Bamoun sultanate palace of 1917, "Cité des arts", museum): Njissé (Protestant hospital) and Manka verified; Njinka is a chiefdom name.

Bafang (West, Haut-Nkam, hilly, coffee, about 2,085 mm): Banka (cathedral, twin town) and Ndokovi verified; town centre on a valley floor ringed by hills.

## Neighborhood football culture

- Pitches: the standard quartier ground is bare earth, terre battue. The Stade Marion at Cité SIC, Douala, was terre battue until a 2025 synthetic turf renovation. Bamboo or pipe goalposts and scratched lines are common knowledge, not sourced here.
- Tournois de vacances (championnats de vacances): July to September school holidays, in every quartier and village, organized by youth leaders, elites and politicians with permits. Teams carry their locality's name (Dragon d'Evodoula, Saint Joseph de Mokolo). Matches most evenings, commentators on rented speakers in French, English and local languages, whole neighborhoods in attendance, free jerseys and balls from promoters, voter registration and health campaigns on the sidelines. NDAMBA's natural career spine.
- Street football: Samuel Eto'o has described playing street football in Douala as a child.
- Benskin (also bendskin, ben-sikin): the moto-taxi, dominant in Douala, Yaoundé and Maroua, negotiated fares, fast and loud. Background traffic and a sideline hazard.
- Beignets-haricots: fried dough with beans, with bouillie (corn porridge) in the morning; sold from tournes-dos stalls in every neighborhood; eaten before school and matches; Eto'o called it the dish of national pride.
- Colors and light: red laterite dust on white kits, green hills around Yaoundé, grey humid sky over Douala, golden dry-season haze in the North, black sand at Limbe, white sand at Kribi. Douala floods May to October, so mud and standing water are realistic.

## Surface types per region

- Red laterite (terre battue): Centre (Yaoundé), Littoral (Douala inland quarters), West (Bafoussam, Dschang, Bafang), East (Bertoua). Hard and fast when dry, dusty, slick when wet. Widely reported; no soil-science source was checked, so treat the geology as common knowledge.
- Sand: Kribi (white, fine, slow ball) and Limbe (black volcanic, heavy). High bounce absorption.
- Mud: Douala and Ebolowa in the rains; Nylon and New-Bell (Ebolowa) flood-prone. Short passes die, slides travel far.
- Grass: stadiums (Ahmadou Ahidjo, Réunification, Roumdé Adjia, Kouekong) and better school grounds. Fast, true roll.
- Dry dusty hard ground: Garoua and Maroua, November to March, Harmattan haze; very fast roll, high bounce, visibility and stamina penalties.
- Cool highland turf: Bamenda, Buea, Dschang; drizzle and fog, soft but not flooded.
