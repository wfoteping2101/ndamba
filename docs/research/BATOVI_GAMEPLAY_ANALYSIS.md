# Batovi Gameplay Analysis: Pixel Cup Soccer / Ultimate Edition

Research dossier for NDAMBA. Compiled 2026-10-31 from the Steam store page, Batovi's site, mobile and Switch store pages, critic and Steam user reviews and OpenCritic. Everything is paraphrased; unverified claims are flagged. NDAMBA reuses no Batovi sprite, code, character, UI or asset; this document exists only to understand why the game feels good.

## 1. Product facts

- Developer and publisher: BATOVI Games Studio, founded 2005, Montevideo, Uruguay. Seller on Apple is listed as ODT S.A.
- Steam: Early Access July 6, 2021; full release October 14, 2022; USD 16.99; around 885 user reviews at 83 percent positive at time of research. Steam Deck Verified per GamingOnLinux and NineOverTen. Native Linux build and a demo exist.
- Switch: released December 26, 2022, USD 16.99, 657 MB, TV, tabletop and handheld modes.
- iOS / Apple TV / Apple silicon Mac: "Pixel Cup Soccer - Ultimate", USD 4.99, 574 MB, 1.0 in May 2024, 2.0 added Party Mode and a MOD area, 2.1 restructured the Global Cup to 48 nations in 12 groups.
- Critics: Hey Poor Player 4/5, Gaming Professors 8/10, NineOverTen 4/5, Gert Lush Gaming positive. Praise: easy to pick up, arcade atmosphere, Nintendo World Cup and Kunio-kun lineage. Complaints: basic passing and shooting, cheap AI, repetition, hard early career.

## 2. Match structure and pacing

- Modes: Friendly, Competitions (cups and leagues), Career, Penalty Kicks, Party Tournament (3 to 8 players), plus a Modding or Tournament editor area.
- Match length: no store page states the exact duration options. A Metacritic user review confirms that match duration and weather are configurable, and Gert Lush notes a match speed setting from 1 to 3. The exact minute presets are unverified; expect a short default (arcade matches commonly run a few real minutes per half).
- Pacing: marketed as fast arcade action, "learn in one minute, master much later". Reviewers describe matches as quick and hard to put down.
- Weather and pitch state affect play: NineOverTen reports waterlogged pitches shorten ball travel on short passes. This is a direct precedent for NDAMBA surface physics.

## 3. Player movement and the sprint question

Verdict: conflicting evidence, so treat it as "no dedicated run button, with speed expressed through player stats and context".

- The Steam feature list includes "sprinting" among actions alongside dribbling and tackling.
- Gert Lush Gaming's Steam review explicitly lists "No run button" as a con and calls movement simplified.
- NineOverTen says players can sprint but names no button.
- The Nintendo and Apple pages do not mention sprint at all.
- A Steam user review complains of "fighting your players" to steer them, and another says it is hard to tell why possession is lost while dribbling.

The most consistent reading: the Ultimate Edition has no held sprint modifier like FIFA or ISS; acceleration comes from player attributes and a context-driven engine. This is an area NDAMBA deliberately changes (see section 11).

## 4. Passing

- Short pass, through pass and lob pass are the three marketed pass types (Steam, GamingOnLinux). The Apple page phrases it as short and long passes.
- An on-screen arrow from the active player shows pass and shot direction (Gert Lush).
- Pass assist and shot assist are optional toggles; an "Assisted mode" setting exists on every platform.
- Gert Lush calls passing "quite basic" and notes the absence of power passes; a Steam user calls passes awkward. Takeaway: a single-tap pass that always reaches a teammate is readable but can feel flat without a power or weight dimension.

## 5. Shooting

- Shots are charged and aimed (Nintendo, Apple, Steam). The HUD shows a power bar during the charge (Gert Lush).
- Controlled shot (lower power, more accuracy) and lob shot are listed separately.
- Super shots exist as a toggle in the rules settings; reviewers do not describe the trigger, so the exact input is unverified. Screenshots on Batovi's site are titled "super shot", suggesting a visibly exaggerated effect.
- Player skills are a separate marketed feature and feed special animations.

## 6. Ball behavior, animations and set pieces

- Ball is a 2D top-down sprite with height; lobs, headers and overhead kicks imply a simulated vertical axis.
- Named animations: overhead kick (the bicycle kick), scorpion kick, scissor kick, diving header.
- Set pieces: corner kicks and throw-ins are aimed; free kicks and penalties exist; a dedicated Penalty Kicks mode.
- Slide tackles leave a mark on the pitch; failed tackles can leave an opponent stunned (NineOverTen).
- Goal replays with slow motion; Gert Lush praised the slow motion implementation.

## 7. Goalkeeping

- Keepers are AI by default. A manual goalkeeper option exists in the control settings (Gert Lush). No reviewer describes keeper behavior in detail, so dive timing and reach are unverified.

## 8. Opponent AI and difficulty

- Difficulty levels on PC: casual, normal, hardcore, custom (Gert Lush). Levels mostly tune assists and player skill rather than rewriting AI. NineOverTen found AI tough even on easy; Steam users recommend normal after finding easy too easy, while others called the CPU unbalanced or said it takes the ball instantly.
- AI teams have styles: a defensive Italian catenaccio and a possession-based Brazilian tiki-taka are the marketed examples.
- Reviewers report "cheap" moments such as constant double-teaming.
- Foul chance is a 1 to 5 slider; fouls, injuries, yellow and red cards all exist.

## 9. Camera and HUD

- Camera: top-down pixel view with a Zoom Level setting; no reviewer mentions multiple angles, so a single angle with zoom presets is the safe assumption.
- Settings confirmed on all platforms: Zoom Level, Slow motion, Assisted mode, VSync, language.
- HUD: active-player direction arrow, power bar, optional hints, adjustable UI speed. Mouse cursor stays visible with a controller on PC (reviewer complaint).

## 10. Tactics, teams, tournaments, multiplayer, input

- Tactical panel: substitutions, formation, attitude (team mentality).
- Editor: team name, players, kit colors, club flag; tournaments editor; Steam Workshop sharing on PC; MOD area on iOS.
- Competitions: Global Cup, Women's Global Cup, continental cups, Global Cup 1930, OlymPixel Cup (men and women), Pixel Leagues D to A, Pixel Tournament, Liberty Cup and Euro Champ Cup. 15 cups per Gert Lush; some unlock through Career.
- Career: build a club from league D up to A, Country Cup, International Cup, Club Global Cup; you are both general manager and coach; finances, morale, fan opinion, experience points for skills, multiple-choice events, cutscenes; 4 save slots. Early career is considered very hard.
- Local multiplayer: up to 8 players, 4v4 co-op or versus; Steam Remote Play Together for online with friends; no public online matchmaking.
- Input: full controller support with platform glyphs; Steam Deck Verified and uses the back grip buttons; keyboard supported; in-game rebinding is weak (goes through Steam Input, and some users report changes not taken).

## 11. Why it feels good

1. Responsiveness: one tap equals one action; the active player arrow and pass arrow give immediate feedback about intent.
2. Readability: a single top-down angle, chunky pixels, strong color separation, and a direction arrow mean the player never asks "where is my man".
3. Short, configurable matches and quick restarts keep the loop under five minutes.
4. Assist systems (assisted mode, pass and shot assists, difficulty presets that tune assists) let children and casual players score early.
5. Juice: slow-motion replays, pitch marks from slides, stun on failed tackles, named spectacular animations.
6. Pitch state matters (waterlogged pitch), giving matches texture without more buttons.
7. Content breadth: cups, leagues, career, party mode, editor, local 8-player.

Where it falls short (from reviews): no sprint button, flat passing, cheap AI doubling, weak rebinding, hard career start, repetition.

## 12. What NDAMBA takes and what it changes

NDAMBA takes the principles, not the product. Original art, code, characters, UI and audio only.

Takes (as design principles):
- One-tap actions with arrow and power-bar feedback.
- Short default matches (target 2 to 3 minute halves) with configurable length and speed.
- Assist toggles and child-friendly difficulty presets.
- Context-sensitive buttons: the same button passes in possession and tackles out of it.
- Slow-motion goal replays, pitch marks, stun on failed slides.
- Local multiplayer up to 8 on one machine and a team editor.

Changes:
- A dedicated sprint button with a stamina cost, because reviews name its absence as the biggest control gap.
- 5v5 neighborhood matches first, on small pitches, before 11v11.
- Weighted passing: tap for short, hold for power or lob, so passing has a skill dimension.
- Surface physics as a core system, not a weather toggle: laterite dust, sand, mud, grass and dry-season hard ground change ball roll, bounce and player traction.
- Cameroonian identity: neighborhoods as teams, street tournaments as the career spine, Makossa, Bikutsi and Mbolé-inspired original audio.
- Fair AI: no double-team cheats; difficulty changes reaction time and decision quality, not rules.
- In-game rebinding and full gamepad navigation in menus from day one.
