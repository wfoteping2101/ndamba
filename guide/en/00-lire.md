## How to read this guide

Ndamba is a football platform in French and English, born in Cameroon, for the whole chain of the game: the ten-year-old learning to lift their head, their parents, their coach, the analyst who codes matches, the technical director of a top-flight club and the federation preparing its national teams. Around the app there is an agent that works for you (Mola), a method book for coaches (*Le Code Njangi*, The Njangi Code), an illustrated novel for children (*Lionceaux*) and a football game that runs in the browser (*Lionceaux Football*).

That can feel like a lot on a first visit. This guide exists so that it no longer does. It answers three questions, in this order: what Ndamba is, how to use every screen, and why all of it can help an African nation lift the World Cup within the next fifteen years.

## Nine parts, twenty-six chapters

- **Part I, Understanding Ndamba**: the platform in ten minutes, the Njangi philosophy, the six profiles and the map of the pages.
- **Part II, Getting started**: the first visit with Mola, the home screen, finding your way and the settings.
- **Part III, Training**: the 64 drills, sessions and the week.
- **Part IV, Team and matches**: the squad, each player's file, preparing a match.
- **Part V, Analysis**: live coding, the report and the Njangi Index, video and the season.
- **Part VI, The player and the family**: the player space, the Lessons of the Ball, the notebook, the journey and parents.
- **Part VII, Mola**: the agent, what it can do and what it never does without you.
- **Part VIII, The books and the game**: *Le Code Njangi*, the *Lionceaux* saga and *Lionceaux Football*.
- **Part IX, Lifting the trophy**: why Africa can win, the 2026 to 2041 plan, the business model, trust and the roadmap.

The appendices gather a quick start for each profile, a glossary, the list of every page, the list of the 64 drills and the most frequent questions.

## The conventions of this guide

- Words in **bold** are the exact labels of the interface: the button, tab or heading you will see on screen. In this edition they are the English labels.
- The screenshots come from the two demos built into Ndamba: the pro club **Étoile de la Côte**, a senior top-flight team in Douala coached by Daniel Mbarga, and the academy **Lionceaux de Bonamoussadi U13**, the team of Kaydoudou and BriBri. These clubs, their players and their matches are invented for the demo. You can open the same demos and follow the guide screen by screen (chapter 5).
- **Tip** boxes give a practical shortcut, **Watch out** boxes flag a trap, **For investors** boxes sum up what a feature changes for the project, and **Ask Mola** boxes give the exact sentence to type so that the agent does it for you.
- The guide describes Ndamba as it is today. When a feature is planned but not there yet, it says so.

## What to know before you start

Today Ndamba runs in the browser, on computers, tablets and phones, with no account to create. Your data (profile, teams, sessions, matches, analyses) stays on your device; you can export a backup and restore it elsewhere (chapter 6). The paid plans are shown, but payments are not connected yet: activating a plan is, for now, a demo (chapter 24).

This guide is read online, chapter by chapter, at **/guide/** on the site, in French and in English. The language button at the top of every page opens the same page in the other edition.
