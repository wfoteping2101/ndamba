## The project

**What is Ndamba, in one sentence?**
A football platform in French and English that helps the whole chain of the game, from the football school to the first team, play with a shared idea, train it every day and check, match after match, whether it really applies it (chapter 1).

**Who is behind Ndamba?**
William Foteping, with his company Lemon Network Solutions. The books *The Njangi Code* and *Lionceaux* are his (chapters 19 and 20).

**Who are Kaydoudou and BriBri?**
The two brothers who are the heroes of the *Lionceaux* novel and of the academy demo. This guide is dedicated to them, and to Nevada (chapter 20).

**Why aim for 2038 rather than 2030?**
Because of age. A player aged 12 to 16 in 2026 will be 20 to 24 in 2034, and 24 to 28 in 2038, the age of full maturity. In 2030 that generation will still be in youth football. The plan keeps all three World Cups open, but sets its targets on the generation trained by the method: a quarter-final or better in 2034, the trophy in 2038 (chapter 23).

**Will Cameroon play the 2026 World Cup?**
No. Beaten 1-0 by DR Congo in the semi-final of the African play-offs, in Rabat, on 14 November 2025, it missed the 2026 edition after eight appearances, the African record (chapter 22).

**Does Ndamba have pilot clubs or partners?**
Not yet. No partnership has been signed to date. The 20 pilot clubs, 100 coaches and 1,000 tagged matches of stage 1 are proposed targets (chapters 23 and 26).

**Do the clubs and people in the demos exist?**
No. Étoile de la Côte, Daniel Mbarga, the Lionceaux de Bonamoussadi and every player in the demos are invented. The only real footballer named in this guide is Roger Milla, for the story of 1990 (chapter 5).

## Using it

**Do I need to create an account?**
No. Ndamba runs in the browser, with no account and no password. You set up your space by talking with Mola, or you open a demo (chapter 5).

**Where is my data?**
On your device, in your browser. It goes nowhere. To keep it or move it to another device, export a backup from **Settings**, then restore it elsewhere (chapters 6 and 25).

**Can I work on two devices at the same time?**
Not yet. Sync between devices will come with accounts (chapter 26). Until then, tag each match on one device only, and carry the backup from one device to the other. A restore replaces everything the browser holds.

**Does Ndamba work without a network?**
Once the app has loaded, mostly yes. Live tagging saves the clock, the tags and the score on the device at every tap, even with no network, and Mola understands your sentences with rules that run on the device (chapters 12 and 18).

**How do I start again from scratch?**
In **Settings**, **Erase everything** deletes the profile, the teams and everything else from this browser. Export a backup first if you want to keep your work (chapter 6).

**In which languages?**
The app is in French and English: the FR and EN button switches the whole interface. The books are read in French. Other languages will come with the federations (chapters 4 and 26).

## Mola

**Does Mola send messages for me?**
Never. Everything that must leave the app, a call-up by WhatsApp, a text message, an e-mail or a calendar file, waits in the **To approve** tab. When you approve, your own app opens with the message ready, and you send it yourself (chapter 18).

**Does Mola use artificial intelligence?**
First a rules engine that runs on the device, in French and English. When the server has a key, an AI model can help understand difficult sentences. It then receives your message, cut to 600 characters, and a short context, never your squad, your notes or your videos (chapters 18 and 25).

## Analysis

**What is the Njangi Index?**
A match score out of 100 that answers one question: did the team possess with purpose? It is the sum of five sub-indices out of 20: Progression, Penetration, Fixation, Regain and Control (chapter 13).

**Are the Index thresholds official benchmarks?**
No. The bands (35, 50, 65 and 80) come from the book, and the report says so itself: the benchmarks are internal to the method, to be calibrated on your own matches. Building benchmarks by age is precisely the purpose of the first 1,000 tagged matches (chapters 13 and 23).

**Can I tag a match on a phone?**
Yes. The live screen adapts to the phone, with large buttons. An assistant, a volunteer parent or a substitute can tag, after reading the table of codes (chapter 12).

**Does video count in the match Index?**
No. The video studio computes its own Index from the tags placed on the video, and is for reviewing, annotating and drawing. The match report and the season are built from live tags, which can also be added after the match (chapter 14).

## Young players and families

**Are children's data protected?**
They stay on the coach's or the family's device, with no account and nothing sent. With young players, the Index measures the team, never a child. The principles to follow on the day accounts arrive (parents' consent, everyone sees only what concerns them) are set out in chapter 25.

**From what age?**
The *Lionceaux* novel is for ages 8 to 12. In the app, the player space changes at 16: before, today's challenge, the lesson of the day, progress and the notebook; from 16, a performance space with the match week, form and load (chapters 15 and 20).

**How does a parent follow their child?**
With the **For parents** box on the home screen, the novel read together, and, if the coach shares a backup, the child's journey. Sharing a single record will come with accounts (chapter 17).
