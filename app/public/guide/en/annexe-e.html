Appendix E

Questions and answers

The questions investors, coaches and parents ask most often, with a short answer and the chapter that goes into detail.

These answers are short. Each one points to the chapter that explains the subject in detail.

The project

What is Ndamba, in one sentence? A football platform in French and English that helps the whole chain of the game, from the football school to the first team, play with a shared idea, train it every day and check, match after match, whether it really applies it (chapter 1).

Who is behind Ndamba? William Foteping, with his company Lemon Network Solutions. The books The Njangi Code and Lionceaux are his (chapters 19 and 20).

Who are Kaydoudou and BriBri? The two brothers who are the heroes of the Lionceaux novel and of the academy demo. This guide is dedicated to them, and to Nevada (chapter 20).

Why aim for 2038 rather than 2030? Because of age. A player aged 12 to 16 in 2026 will be 20 to 24 in 2034, and 24 to 28 in 2038, the age of full maturity. In 2030 that generation will still be in youth football. The plan keeps all three World Cups open, but sets its targets on the generation trained by the method: a quarter-final or better in 2034, the trophy in 2038 (chapter 23).

Will Cameroon play the 2026 World Cup? No. Beaten 1-0 by DR Congo in the semi-final of the African play-offs, in Rabat, on 14 November 2025, it missed the 2026 edition after eight appearances, the African record (chapter 22).

Does Ndamba have pilot clubs or partners? Not yet. No partnership has been signed to date. The 20 pilot clubs, 100 coaches and 1,000 tagged matches of stage 1 are proposed targets (chapters 23 and 26).

Do the clubs and people in the demos exist? No. Étoile de la Côte, Daniel Mbarga, the Lionceaux de Bonamoussadi and every player in the demos are invented. The only real footballer named in this guide is Roger Milla, for the story of 1990 (chapter 5).

Using it

Do I need to create an account? No. Ndamba runs in the browser, with no account and no password. You set up your space by talking with Mola, or you open a demo (chapter 5).

Where is my data? On your device, in your browser. Nothing goes anywhere else, except the text you write to Mola when its AI model is active. To keep it or move it to another device, export a backup from Settings, then restore it elsewhere (chapters 6 and 25).

Can I work on two devices at the same time? Not yet. Sync between devices will come with accounts (chapter 26). Until then, tag each match on one device only, and carry the backup from one device to the other. A restore replaces everything the browser holds.

Does Ndamba work without a network? Partly. You need a network to open the site and to load each screen the first time: there is no installable offline mode yet. After that, since the data is on the device, an open screen keeps working if the network drops. Live tagging saves the clock, the tags and the score at every tap, even with no network, and Mola understands your sentences with rules that run on the device (chapters 12 and 18).

How do I start again from scratch? In Settings, Erase everything deletes the profile, the teams and everything else from this browser. Export a backup first if you want to keep your work (chapter 6).

In which languages? The app is in French and English: the FR and EN button switches the whole interface. The books are read in French. More languages are planned, at the pace of the federations that join the model (chapters 4 and 26).

The plans

How much does Ndamba cost? Free: 0. Player Premium: US$4.99 a month. Coach Pro: US$12.99 a month. Club and academy: US$49 a month. Federation: on quote. Payments are not connected yet: activating a plan is, for now, a demo (chapter 24).

How much does the AI analysis of a match cost? It is proposed at US$2.99 per match, on top of any plan. That price is a proposal to confirm: it roughly covers what the model’s reading of the frames costs, about US$2.5 for a 90-minute match at one frame every 6 seconds. Since payments are not connected, access goes through a code given after payment, and the option only works once the site owner has set the key (chapters 14 and 24).

What does the free plan include? The two books to read freely, the 64 drills of the method, Mola to guide you, five sessions and one analysed match (on video or live). Beyond that, the app suggests Coach Pro. Both limits also apply to Player Premium, which opens the player’s space, not the coach’s tools (chapter 24).

Are some plan lines ahead of the app? Yes. Two lines of Club and academy carry the words "coming soon": staff accounts, which need user accounts, on the roadmap, and the club game model, which today is built through support work. Under Federation, coach education and support are services delivered by people (chapters 24 and 26).

Mola

Does Mola send messages for me? Never. Everything that must leave the app, a call-up by WhatsApp, a text message, an e-mail or a calendar file, waits in the To approve tab. When you approve, your own app opens with the message ready, and you send it yourself (chapter 18).

Does Mola use artificial intelligence? First a rules engine that runs on the device, in French and English. When the server has a key, an AI model can help understand difficult sentences. It then receives your message, cut to 600 characters, and a short context, never your squad, your notes or your videos (chapters 18 and 25).

Analysis

What is the Njangi Index? A match score out of 100 that answers one question: did the team possess with purpose? It is the sum of five sub-indices out of 20: Progression, Penetration, Fixation, Regain and Control (chapter 13).

Are the Index thresholds official benchmarks? No. The bands (35, 50, 65 and 80) come from the book, and the report says so itself: the benchmarks are internal to the method, to be calibrated on your own matches. Building benchmarks by age is precisely the purpose of the first 1,000 tagged matches (chapters 13 and 23).

Is the analysis done by hand? Yes, and on purpose. A person watches the match and taps a button for each action: completed pass, pass that breaks a line, loss, ball won back within five seconds, shot. The recommended way is to do it on the video: each code keeps its moment, so it can be watched again, checked and corrected, in slow motion if needed. That is how analysts work, and it is what gives the Njangi Index. The paid AI analysis adds estimates drawn from the frames (possession, territory, block, rest defence, moments to watch again), but does not count every pass. Live tagging from the touchline remains possible for a match that is not filmed, with nothing to watch again afterwards. Automatic detection of every action is on the roadmap (chapters 12, 14 and 26).

Why not upload the video straight to Ndamba? Because a match video weighs several gigabytes: storing and streaming it for every club would cost a lot in servers and bandwidth, and that cost would fall on the clubs. So Ndamba uses what already exists. You put the video on YouTube as "unlisted" or on Facebook, and you paste its link: Ndamba keeps only the link. Or you open the file on the device, filmed with the phone for example: it is never sent, but it has to be reopened on each visit (chapter 12).

Can I tag a match on a phone? Yes. The video studio and the live screen adapt to the phone, with large buttons, and the studio’s Film button opens the camera. An assistant, a volunteer parent or a substitute can tag, after reading the table of codes (chapter 12).

Does video count in the match Index? Yes. When a video is linked to a match, every code placed in the studio goes into that match’s report, with its moment in the video: the Index, the report and the season are built from those codes. There is only one tagging per match: if the match was first tagged live, the studio offers to replace those codes with the video’s, after a confirmation. Notes and drawings stay in the studio (chapters 13 and 14).

Young players and families

Are children’s data protected? They stay on the coach’s or the family’s device, with no account and nothing sent. Videos are not stored by Ndamba: for minors, prefer unlisted YouTube or a file kept on the device, and the AI analysis only sends frames after a ticked box confirming the agreement of the players and their parents. With young players, the Index measures the team, never a child, and the report asks the staff to keep the player contributions table to themselves. The principles to follow on the day accounts arrive (parents’ consent, everyone sees only what concerns them) are set out in chapter 25.

From what age? The Lionceaux novel is for ages 8 to 12. In the app, the player space changes at 16: before, today’s challenge, the lesson of the day, progress and the notebook; from 16, a performance space with the match week, form and load (chapters 15 and 20).

How does a parent follow their child? With the For parents box on the home screen, today’s challenges and the novel read together. The journey written by the coach stays on the team’s device for now: a backup would hold the whole squad, not just the child. Sharing a single record will come with accounts (chapters 17 and 25).
