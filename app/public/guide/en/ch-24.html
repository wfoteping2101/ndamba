Chapter 24

The business model

The five plans and their prices, the AI analysis paid per match, who pays and why, the rule that keeps education free, the two limits of the free plan, the real state of payments, the running costs and revenue scenarios presented as hypotheses.

12 min read

A business model says who pays, for what, and what it all costs. Ndamba’s starts from a conviction of the method: you do not build a shared football culture by charging for knowledge. The books and the drills are therefore free for everyone. What people pay for are the tools that save time and measure: preparing, analysing, following players, and Mola acting for you. This chapter presents the five plans as the Premium page shows them, what the app actually enforces today and what it does not enforce yet. It ends with figures that are arithmetic hypotheses, not forecasts.

The starting rule: education stays free

The app’s configuration file states the rule in plain terms: the books and the drill library are never behind a paywall. Whatever the plan:

  • The Njangi Code (36 chapters in nine parts) and Lionceaux, book 1: Le Ballon qui voyage (40 chapters) are free to read online;
  • the method’s 64 drills, with their animations and coaching points, stay open in Drills;
  • the 40 Ball Lessons, taken from the novel, stay open too.

The Premium page confirms it in its questions: "Are the books really free? Yes." The reason is strategic as much as it is moral. A coach at a club with no budget must be able to learn the method. The more coaches speak the same language, the more valuable the tools that measure it become.

The Premium page is called "A plan for every role". It shows prices in US dollars, with an indicative FCFA equivalent, and a Monthly or Yearly switch. Yearly billing means ten months paid: that is what the "2 months free" label means.

The page computes the FCFA amounts at about 600 FCFA to the dollar, rounded to the nearest hundred, and says they are a guide. With yearly billing it also shows the monthly equivalent: $4.16 for Player Premium, $10.83 for Coach Pro, $40.83 for Club and academy. Coach Pro carries the label "Recommended for coaches".

Each plan lists what it unlocks:

Plan What the Premium page lists

Below the plans, a separate card presents the AI match analysis: "On top of any plan", $2.99 per match, with its FCFA equivalent. It is described further down.

Who pays, and why

Players, and often their parents. Player Premium is aimed at players of every age. For a child aged 8 to 12, it is a fifteen-minute daily routine (the challenges), the novel’s lessons and a notebook. For an older player, it is the journey, match week and annotated videos. What they buy is visible progress and a habit.

Coaches. Coach Pro is the recommended plan, and the coach is the engine of the model: the method reaches players through the coach. What the coach buys is time and a measure: a week of sessions built around the match, a match tagged from the touchline that produces a report straight away, Mola preparing the call-up.

Clubs and academies. Club and academy covers every age group and several teams. What the club buys is continuity inside its own walls: the same model from U8 to the first team, and a journey that follows each player from one age group to the next.

Federations and leagues. This plan is priced on quote. The Premium page puts it this way: "The national game model, coach education and support are built together with you. The price is set on quote." What a federation buys: one measure across all its national teams, trained coaches and age references. This is stage 3 of the plan (chapter 23).

One point needs to be clear for an investor. Some lines describe what the plan must deliver rather than what the app does today, and the page flags two of them with the words "coming soon". "Staff accounts (coming soon)" require user accounts, which are planned but do not exist yet. "Your club game model (coming soon)" and the "National game model" are today the model of The Njangi Code, which shapes the drills, the sessions and the Index; adapting it to a club or a nation is support work, not a screen. Coach education and dedicated support are services delivered by people.

The free plan and its two limits

In today’s code, the Free plan has exactly two limits, and they hold everywhere in the app. They also apply to Player Premium: that plan opens the player’s space, not the coach’s tools.

  1. Five sessions. Beyond that, the Sessions page and the New session window show "Your 5 free sessions are used", with a Discover Coach Pro button; the Week page shows the same box and its Plan the week button stays greyed out. The Prepare the week button on the home screen and Plan the week with these objectives in the match report then open the Premium page. Mola applies the same rule when you ask it for a session or for the week: "Your free plan already has 5 sessions. For me to keep building them, move to Coach Pro. The books and the 64 drills stay open to everyone."
  2. One analysed match, on video or live. The Free card says "One analysed match, on video or live": the rule counts any tagged match, whatever the method. Once one match has been tagged, no other can be. The match sheet shows "Unlimited analysis is in Coach Pro" and a See plans link, and its Add the match video button carries a padlock; the Analysis page replaces Add the video and the live button with See plans, and the live page itself shows the same message if you open it from its address. A match that already has tags is never locked: you can always review and fix it.

Everything else is unlimited today, even when the Premium page places it in a paid plan: the number of teams (the free plan lists "One team"), reports, video, the lessons or the notebook. The page describes the planned offer; for now the code enforces only those two thresholds. They will need to be aligned with the page when payments arrive.

The AI analysis, paid per match

Analysing a match rests on tagging: a person taps a button for each action, on the video (chapter 12). The AI match analysis, in the video studio (chapter 14), adds an automatic reading of the frames. Claude, Anthropic’s AI, reads one frame every 4, 6 or 10 seconds of the match video file and estimates possession, the zone where the ball is, the height of the block without the ball and the rest defence, with the moments to watch again and a short written summary. It does not replace tagging and does not change the Njangi Index.

It is a paid option, on top of any plan, because it has a direct cost for the platform, every match:

Item Order of magnitude
One 960 x 540 pixel frame About 700 tokens read by the model
A 90-minute match, one frame every 6 seconds About 900 frames, so about 630,000 tokens
Opus 5.5 public price for tokens read $4 per million, according to Anthropic’s documentation
Cost of a match’s frames About $2.5, plus the model’s written reply
Price proposed to the user $2.99 per match

The $2.99 price is a proposal to confirm, not a final tariff: the margin is thin, and the cost drops with one frame every 10 seconds or a cheaper model (the site owner can set another one). Since payments are not connected, access goes through an access code given after payment, which the user types in the studio. For the option to work, the site owner must enter, in the hosting settings, the Anthropic API key (ANTHROPIC_API_KEY) and the list of valid codes (ANALYSE_CODES), and if wanted the model (ANTHROPIC_VISION_MODEL). Until that is done, the studio says the AI analysis is not activated on this site yet. A code has no quota yet: in-app payment and a number of matches per code are on the roadmap (chapter 26).

The real state: payments are not connected

A banner at the top of the Premium page says it plainly: "Payments are not connected yet. Mobile Money and cards are coming soon. Meanwhile, you can activate any plan in demo mode and try everything, without paying."

To activate a plan in demo mode:

  1. Open Premium, in the app menu (the Me group) or on the site.
  2. Choose Monthly or Yearly to see the matching prices.
  3. Tap Activate (demo) under the plan you want.
  4. A message confirms it, for example: "Coach Pro plan activated in demo mode. No payment was taken."
  5. The plan’s card now shows Your plan, and the Plan panel in Settings shows it under Current plan.

Technically, this button writes the plan’s name into the profile saved on the device, and nothing else: no payment, no server, no account. Both demos open directly on Coach Pro, so that everything can be tried. A space created through Mola’s welcome conversation starts on Free. Without a profile, the page shows Get started instead of the activation button.

Watch out
No money is collected today

Activate (demo) charges nothing and commits no one. As a result, Ndamba collects no revenue today. The prices shown are there to present and test the offer, not to measure sales.

What it costs to run

The choice of a "local first" app, where data stays on the device, keeps today’s running costs very light. Here are the cost lines, described without amounts because they depend on usage:

  • Hosting. The platform is a static site: the app, both books with their illustrations and the Lionceaux Football game (about 25 MB on first launch, according to its page). There is no database server. Cost mainly follows traffic.
  • Mola’s optional AI model. This is one of the two server functions, at /api/mola. When the key is configured, most messages typed to Mola go to the Anthropic API (the default model is claude-haiku-5-5): the message, cut to 600 characters, a set of instructions and a short context, for a reply capped at 400 tokens. Chapter 25 lists the exceptions. Cost follows the number of messages. Without a key, the function replies that it is not configured, the rules engine answers on its own, and this line costs nothing.
  • The optional AI match analysis. The second server function, at /api/analyse, receives frames of a match and passes them to the Anthropic API. Its cost, about $2.5 per match at one frame every 6 seconds, is covered by the price asked for each match (see above). Without a key and codes, it does nothing and this line costs nothing.
  • Costs to come. Accounts and sync (servers, storage, security), payment provider fees, support, coach education, and the writing and illustration of books 2 to 6 of the saga.

Today’s lightness has a counterpart: backups are the user’s job (chapter 25).

The levers of growth

  • The free way in. The books, the 64 drills and two full demos, open without an account, let people discover the method before buying anything.
  • The coach as the entry point. A convinced coach brings a team, its players and their parents, and then the club.
  • From club to league. When a league adopts the shared model, every club in it has a reason to use the tool.
  • Two languages from day one. French and English, for French-speaking and English-speaking Africa and for the diaspora; more languages are planned.
  • Prices designed for Africa. Amounts shown in FCFA, yearly billing at ten months, and Mobile Money announced.
  • Mola. Time saved is the easiest argument for a coach to check.
  • The saga and the game. Lionceaux (six books planned, the first one published) and Lionceaux Football bring children and families to the same vocabulary.

The risks are known: what clubs and families can afford, the cost of mobile data, and payments that are not connected yet.

For investors
Where the model stands

The offer is defined (five plans, prices in dollars and FCFA), the paid tools work, education is free, and current costs are low thanks to local storage. What is missing to collect money: payments, then accounts. The first signals to watch will be the number of coaches active every week, the move from Free to Coach Pro once payments are connected, and a first quote with a league or a federation.
